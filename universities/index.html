For each one: its strongest education work, and any response to portable K–12 funding. The limit, first. These lists measure research standing and reputation, not teacher production. In Florida, about two in three new certified teachers in 2023–24 came from programs outside both lists: regional universities, state colleges, district routes and private providers. UWF, FAU and UNF alone finished 577 and are on neither list. Nationally, Western Governors University, which calls itself the largest school of education in the nation, is on neither. 01

Three lists, one answer

Each bar is one group, split by how its institutions respond. The Florida bar counts every Florida institution, state colleges included; the two national lists count universities only. Hover a segment for names.

About half show no response we could find.

Being ranked for innovation does not predict a response: 45% of the Most Innovative respond, against 54% of the AAU. Twelve of the 74 run, founded or authorize K–12 schools. Three prepare educators for Catholic schools: Boston College, Marquette and Santa Clara.

Seven of the 102 are in Florida. The Preparation Index goes deeper there: 46 Florida institutions, program by program, including state colleges.

03

Four ways to respond

The responding universities, read through four lenses. A filled dot is a verified signal. Tap a row for the evidence.

Tap a university to see its evidence.
Lens 1 · Teacher prep beyond public schools

It exists, for older sectors

Catholic, independent and Jewish schools have university pipelines. Microschools, hybrid schools, homeschool co-ops and ESA providers do not.

  • Penn's residency places fellows in 21 independent schools
  • George Mason trains independent-school leaders
Lens 2 · Services to non-public students

The most direct response

Universities run, found or authorize K–12 schools: charter schools, private lab schools and online schools. Some sit inside the education school (USC, Pitt, UNC), others outside it (ASU, UC San Diego, Boston University).

  • ASU Prep Digital takes Arizona ESA funds
  • Auburn is a registered Alabama ESA provider
Lens 3 · Market and vendor behavior

Big vendors, to states

Universities sell assessments and curricula to states and districts, not to families spending portable funds.

  • UF Lastinger vetted tutoring vendors for Florida
  • Kansas, Wisconsin and Oregon sell assessments to states
Lens 4 · Campus debate

Faculty-led, at the poles

Debate lives in a few research centers. No education school was found taking an institutional position on ESAs.

  • Tulane REACH studies choice; CU Boulder's NEPC critiques it
  • Johns Hopkins maps homeschooling

The response lives outside the education school.

University-run schools, online academies, gifted centers and statewide institutes are closer to portable funds than any college of education.
