What it measures. Not whether institutions should respond — whether and how they have. Building a program and declining to build one are both coded observations. The Index is written so that supporters and skeptics of these policies can cite the same rows.

What makes it different. Catalog scans miss the main event: where universities have entered this sector, they've done it by operating and authorizing schools, not by listing courses. The Index codes both.

The seam

A Florida district needed a school model. It bought one from a microschool operator.

Polk County contracted a private microschool founded by two teachers who had left a Polk County public school. The Index has coded 21 Florida institutions and found no education-school program built for this sector. Both facts describe the same state in the same year.

Field Note 09 · August 2026
Institutions

Three-quarters of Florida districts now sell to students they do not enroll

District participation in Florida's part-time scholarship marketplace went from roughly one in three to about 75% of 67 districts in twelve months. Texas districts, offered state money for a comparable arrangement, mostly declined. The contrast is the finding.

Field Note 08 · August 2026
The seam

In much of the Americas, the money follows the teacher

Coding North and South America turned up a mechanism the school-choice literature barely names: public money paid not per pupil, but against a private school's teaching payroll. Five systems in this hemisphere work that way.

Corrections on the front page

When we're wrong, the correction runs here, in this color, with what changed and why. Both of ours are below, newest first, permanently.

Correction 02 · August 2026

We coded the University of West Florida "nothing found." It is a registered charter school sponsor. Our July pass read UWF's School of Education roster, found nothing built for the sector, and coded the institution Tier 0. UWF is listed in the Florida Department of Education's own Directory of Charter School Sponsoring Entities, and its trustees approved it as authorizer of a Somerset Academy charter school. The row is recoded Tier 1.

The error matters more than the row. This Index's central methodological claim is that catalog scans miss the main event, because universities enter this sector by operating and authorizing schools rather than by listing courses. We made exactly that mistake, in our most heavily covered state. The state's own sponsor directory is now part of the method.

Correction 01 · July 2026

An early draft of our baseline claimed no prior systematic scan of colleges of education existed. EdChoice published one in 2023 — Surfing the Pipeline (McShane), with Hanover Research. Our baseline now builds on it, with attribution. The error and this note are permanent parts of the record.

Why it matters to the US question

The Netherlands has had the most pluralistic provider system in the world for over a century — and still entered a structural teacher shortage. England moved credentialing off the university campus on purpose, and supply fell. Portability does not produce workforce sufficiency on its own. That is the finding the American debate has not yet absorbed.
