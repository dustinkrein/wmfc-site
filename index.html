Who counts as an educator is widening.

About 4 million tutors, coaches, mentors and afterschool staff work alongside classroom teachers. Arizona State's education college now treats them as one youth-serving workforce, and its September 2026 report asks universities to build degrees, certificates and stackable credentials for it. Portable funding pays for many of these roles directly.

Florida's colleges prepare 60% fewer teachers. Most of the drop came before universal choice.

People who finished a state-approved program leading to a Florida teaching certificate, by who ran the program. Dashed lines mark Florida's first ESA and universal eligibility.

Source: U.S. Dept. of Education, Title II state data files, Florida, 2010–11 to 2023–24. 2016–17 is left out: the federal file double-counts it. Policy markers: SB 850 (2014), first ESA; HB 1 (2023), universal eligibility. Data

Most of the decline came before universal choice.

College completers fell from 7,406 to 3,453 by 2022–23, the last year before HB 1 made every student eligible. That is 89% of the total drop. The whole pipeline fell 53%, not only colleges, while U.S. teacher-prep enrollment fell about a third. The timing does not support reading the decline as a response to portable funding.

The largest teacher preparer in Florida is not a university.

District and charter certification programs, counted together as one route, finished 657 teachers in 2023–24. The largest single university, UCF, finished 392. Programs outside colleges went from 8% of new teachers to 22%, mostly because colleges shrank: their own count rose only from 652 to 819.

A charter-school service firm out-produced the flagship.

Noble Education Initiative, which serves more than 100 charter schools, finished 116 Florida teachers. The University of Florida finished 106.

Education degrees fell a third. Certified completers fell more.

Florida education bachelor's degrees fell 36% from 2011–12 to 2023–24. FIU awarded 433 of them in 2023–24; 65 people finished its teacher program. NCES IPEDS, 2023–24 provisional

The limit, first. These counts cover only programs that lead to a Florida certificate. Private-school and microschool teachers who need no certificate, and Floridians trained by out-of-state online programs, do not appear. Neither source records where completers go on to teach.

Where Florida institutions engage, they sponsor schools. They do not build programs.

All five Florida institutions that are in the sector are charter sponsors: two universities and three state colleges. FSU charters its own three lab schools; it does not use them to prepare educators for this sector. UF runs a lab school that is not a charter.

Holding a license is the line. Nothing in the Index prepares educators for the unlicensed slice.

That slice is microschools, hybrid schools, homeschool co-ops and ESA providers: the fastest-growing part of the market.

Field Notes

Short findings between editions. Each has a permanent address.

Workforce

On January 1, a federal tax credit starts paying for tutoring. No university prepares tutors for it.

The new federal scholarship tax credit can cover tutoring and after-school programs for public school students, not only private tuition. Thirty-one states have opted in. The workforce it pays for has no degree path.

Field Note 17 · October 2026
Workforce

Who prepares the teachers of microschools, classical schools and Christian schools? Mostly organizations that publish no counts.

We found twelve organizations preparing educators for the schools portable funding pays for. Three publish a number of people prepared. One reports to a state.

Field Note 16 · October 2026
Institutions

On the ESA vendor lists, universities are a rounding error.

Texas lists 5,648 approved vendors for its new ESA program. About ten belong to colleges, and most of those are campus child-care centers.

Field Note 15 · October 2026
The seam

Nine campus centers study the new schools. None prepares their teachers.

Research on hybrid schools, homeschooling and microschools now has a home on campus. Preparing the people who teach in them does not.

Correction 05 · October 2026

Our headline numbers disagreed across pages, we missed a Florida charter sponsor, and we called Florida complete when it was not.

A review found four errors. The Universities page header said 32 universities respond and 6 compete; the data said 49 and 18. We coded Florida State University “nothing found” though it charters three lab schools. We called Florida coded in full when only 3 of 28 state colleges had been read. And our Florida teacher-supply chart did not say that 89% of the college decline came before universal choice. All four are fixed, and the Index now reports preparing educators and running schools separately. Read the correction

Correction 04 · October 2026

We said about two in three universities showed no response. After a second search, it is about half.

Our first pass searched mostly education schools. A second search recoded 17 of 70 universities, 12 of them for running, founding or authorizing schools. No response found is now 46% of the AAU and 55% of the Most Innovative. All corrections
