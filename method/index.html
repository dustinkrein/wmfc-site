02

How responses are coded

Every response gets a direction. The direction records what an institution did, not whether it was right.

CompeteBuilds or runs an alternative that takes portable funds
SupplySells services or preparation to whoever is buying
FortifyStrengthens the existing public system
Open doorRemoves a barrier for educators headed outside districts
Studies itResearches or debates the policy without acting in the market
Searched, none foundLooked for, not found. Never "nothing there"
The Preparation Index

Four categories, by what was built

  • Built for the sector: stated purpose is to prepare, credential, support or operate education outside district schools. Reported in two parts that are never added together in a headline: prepares educators (a degree, certificate or residency built for the sector) and runs or authorizes schools (in the sector, but not preparing its workforce).
  • Names the sector: institutional text names private, charter or non-traditional settings.
  • Incidental: reaches these educators only as a side effect.
  • Nothing found: absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

Operating and authorizing schools is coded, not just programs, because that is where most institutions entered the sector. Running a school is not preparing its teachers, which is why the two are counted separately. Called the Readiness Index until 31 October 2026; renamed because it counts what was built, not whether a workforce is ready (Correction 05). Before any institution is coded "nothing found," the state's charter sponsor registry is checked.

The university scan

Innovation score, and why it is separate

Each university's two strongest education initiatives are scored on scale, evidence and novelty (0–5 each). Score = 2 × novelty + scale + evidence, max 20. The portfolio is the mean of the top two; a missing second counts as zero. Response to portable funding is coded separately, so innovation never stands in for response.

03

Three evidence states

Most of what this research finds is absence. Absence is drawn, never left blank.

DocumentedFound, with a primary source and the date a person read it.
Searched, none foundWe looked, by a stated method, and there was nothing. A finding, not a gap.
Not yet checkedNobody has looked. Honest, countable, and the next item of work.

Each row in the university scan carries its state in the data (evidence_state): 49 documented, 50 searched with none found, 3 unresolved, 0 not yet checked. In the Preparation Index, “nothing found” is the searched state, and states read only flagship-first say so in their coverage note.

How a row is found

Same places, same order, every time

  • 1. Registries first. The state’s charter sponsor or authorizer registry, and any ESA provider list. Added after Correction 02: a course list cannot show what a board of trustees has authorized.
  • 2. The education unit. Program and degree listings, then catalog course descriptions, then named centers and institutes.
  • 3. The rest of the institution. K–12, online, lab-school, charter and outreach units. Added after Correction 03, when a microschool fellowship sat outside the education school.
  • 4. News. For moves the institution’s own pages do not describe.

Terms searched against each unit’s site and catalog: microschool, learning pod, homeschool support, ESA or education savings account, school choice, education entrepreneurship, private school leadership, classical education, charter school leadership, non-traditional settings, hybrid school. For every hit, the sentence and its URL are kept; the strongest one becomes the row’s evidence.

Decision rule. Work down from the strongest category and assign the first one the text supports. When a row sits between two categories, code the lower one. Over-coding engagement is the error that costs the most credibility.

Who codes, and what that cannot catch

One coder per row, no reliability figure

Each row is coded by one researcher. Searching and first drafts are AI-assisted; every quoted passage is checked against its live source before the row is published, and the check date is the date that happened. No second coder independently re-codes rows, so there is no inter-rater reliability figure. What catches errors instead is the second pass: five of the six corrections so far came from re-reading work already published.

Innovation scores are rougher than the Index. Novelty, scale and evidence are scored 0–5 by judgment against the other scored rows. There is no written anchor for each point on each scale. Use the scores to sort, not to rank closely.

Not done: interviews, archived catalogs, and requests to institutions. A program that was considered and declined leaves no published trace.

04

Limits that bind hard

  • Cases were found by searching for engagement. That samples on the outcome and biases every explanation offered.
  • Coverage is uneven. Michigan is complete. In Florida every public institution is read one by one: all State University System education units and all 28 state colleges. Florida’s private colleges are read for 7 of the 25 with state-approved preparation programs. The other universal-ESA states are read flagship-first: the flagship, the original normal school, and anything on the state’s charter-authorizer or ESA provider list. Four states carry a single placeholder row. Read every state total against its depth.
  • Published text only. Coding rests on program pages, catalogs and news, not interviews. An institution that considered a program and declined looks the same as one that never considered it.
  • The university lists measure research standing and reputation, not teacher production. AAU membership and the U.S. News Most Innovative list leave out most of the institutions that prepare teachers: regional public universities, state colleges and large online providers. In Florida, UWF, FAU and UNF finished 577 certified teachers in 2023–24 and are on neither list. The Preparation Index reads institutions like these one by one; the university scan does not.
  • The university scan is a first pass. 102 universities, public sources from 2025–2026. "Searched, none found" means two passes found nothing. Three rows (UC Davis, Miami, Howard) are unresolved and count as none until re-checked. The 31 Most Innovative universities outside the AAU are coded for response but not yet scored.
  • State colleges are read at program-page depth. The 25 Florida College System institutions added on 31 October 2026 were read from program pages, state approval files and news, not course catalogs. A college-run charter school is found by searching the college’s own pages, district charter lists and the 2021 state list of college-operated charters; a newer one could be missed.
  • Single points in time. This field moves fast. Every row carries the date it was checked.

Corrections

What was wrong, what is right, and what it changes. Dated and permanent. No silent edits.

Correction 08 · November 2026

We missed a twelfth route into teaching, called UWF’s placement policy silent when its catalog names private schools, and coded UNF open from a list that is not a placement policy.

Three errors, found on 3 November 2026 while building a census of all 40 Florida public universities and state colleges. 1. A missing route. The homepage and What we know said all 11 of Florida’s teacher certification routes end in a district, a charter school or a charter network. Since 1 July 2024 Florida has also issued a restricted Classical Education Teaching Certificate, requested by a classical school and valid only at one. Eleven of twelve routes end in a district, charter or charter network. The state’s description does not say whether a classical school must be public, and we have not established it. Both pages now say eleven of twelve. 2. UWF recoded from silent to open. Our September placement survey read the University of West Florida’s placement page, which names only counties. Its catalog says student teaching takes place “in a public or private school.” 3. UNF recoded from open in practice to silent. The partner list we cited, which names KIPP Jacksonville, belongs to UNF’s Florida Institute of Education, not to student teaching. We found no published UNF placement policy. The placement survey now counts 4 of 10 programs as open, not 3; UNF’s Preparation Index row is unchanged at Incidental, with its evidence reworded. A change in how we report, not an error: a new page, Teacher prep in Florida, codes all 40 public institutions from their own pages. Every quoted line was read on its live page on the date shown, and our preflight check fails if the page and its dataset disagree.

Correction 07 · November 2026

Field Note 17 said 31 states opted in to the federal scholarship tax credit. The IRS list shows 30.

One error, found in a review on 1 November 2026. Field Note 17 took its count of participating states from press reporting (Education Week, 14 October). The IRS list of participating states, the official record, shows 30 as of 22 October 2026. The note now cites the IRS list and carries a dated correction; we could not identify which state accounts for the difference. Changes in how we report, not errors: the About page now names the author, Dustin J. Krein, Ed.D., and states that the project is self-funded with no outside money; it also says plainly that data rows are built to be cited by supporters and skeptics alike, while headlines and Field Notes state conclusions. The main menu is trimmed to six items, with the Atlas, the Navigator and About in the footer. The Preparation Index and university tables now print their rows into the page, so they can be read without JavaScript, and our preflight check fails if those rows fall out of date.

Correction 06 · November 2026

We said 53 Florida districts sell to scholarship students. Our source said approved or applying. And we said most universities had not responded; about half have.

Two errors, found in a review on 1 November 2026. 1. Overstated district count. The homepage and Districts page said 53 of 67 Florida districts sell part-time services to scholarship students. Our own July 2026 source counted districts approved or in process. Both pages now say “approved or applying,” and name the source for what it is: Step Up For Students, which administers the scholarships, writing on its own blog. We found no independent state count. 2. A stale line. The Districts page said most universities have not responded at all. Since Correction 04, about half of the 102 show a response. Changes in how we report, not errors: the district comparison now sets districts beside colleges as sellers, using Step Up’s 2024–25 PEP spending (colleges took 0.25%); the Preparation Index adds a fifth explanation, that no one may be paying for the credential; the Method page now publishes the search protocol, says who codes and what a single coder cannot catch, and each university row carries its evidence state; the timeline defines what counts as a college response.

Correction 05 · October 2026

Our headline numbers disagreed across pages, we missed a Florida charter sponsor, and we called Florida complete when it was not.

Four errors, found in a review on 31 October 2026. 1. Stale numbers. The Universities page said 32 universities respond and 6 compete. After Correction 04 the data said 49 and 18; the homepage already said 18. The page header was typed by hand and never updated. Every headline number on that page is now computed from the dataset, and our preflight check now fails if any page disagrees with it. 2. A missed sponsor. We coded Florida State University “nothing found.” FSU is listed three times in the state’s charter sponsor registry: it charters its own lab schools in Tallahassee, Pembroke Pines and Panama City. This is the error Correction 02 was meant to prevent. FSU is now coded as running and authorizing schools; no preparation program was found. 3. Coverage overstated. We said Florida was coded in full. Only 3 of its 28 state colleges had been read, though state colleges finished about one in five of Florida’s new certified teachers in 2023–24. All 28 were read on 31 October 2026. Eight more run charter schools, so Florida’s count of institutions in the sector moves from four to 13; none prepares educators for it. Florida stays labeled partial, because only 7 of its 25 private colleges with state-approved programs have been read. We also added Western Governors University, the country’s largest education school by its own account, which both university lists leave out. 4. A causal reading we did not support. Our Florida chart said colleges prepare 60% fewer teachers without saying when. 89% of that drop came before universal eligibility in 2023. The chart now marks the policy dates and says so. A change in how we report, not an error: the Preparation Index category “built for the sector” is now always shown in two parts, preparing educators and running or authorizing schools. Running a school is not preparing its teachers, and a single count blurred that. For the same reason the Readiness Index is now the Preparation Index: the old name implied a measure of workforce readiness, when most of its top category is institutions that run schools. Earlier Field Notes now use the new name.

Correction 04 · October 2026

We said about two in three universities showed no response. After a second search, it is about half.

Our first pass searched mostly education schools. A second search on 28 October 2026, of each “none” row’s K–12, online, charter, lab-school and outreach units, recoded 17 of 70 universities. Twelve run, founded or authorize schools: among them UT Austin’s 24-campus charter district and online high school, UC San Diego’s Preuss School, USC’s Hybrid High, WashU’s sponsorship of KIPP St. Louis and Rutgers–Camden’s LEAP Academy. No response found now stands at 46% of the AAU, 55% of the Most Innovative and five of seven in Florida. We also withdrew the claim that responses sit mostly outside the education school; the recoded schools are split. Each recoded row is marked in the data. Still unresolved: UC Davis, Howard, Miami and a separate pass on district partnerships, which the first pass coded unevenly.

Correction 03 · October 2026

We said no university helps people start or staff a microschool. Arizona State does.

Our first-pass scan coded ASU on ASU Prep Digital's approval as an Arizona ESA vendor and searched for microschool preparation in education schools only. ASU Prep Global, ASU's K–12 unit, runs a Microschool Entrepreneur Fellowship that trains parents, educators and school leaders to open microschools: pedagogy, curriculum, financial modeling, team recruitment, an in-person retreat in Tempe and months of coaching. The homepage now reads 1 of 102. The size of the finding barely moves. Its location does: the one program we found sits outside the education school, the same pattern as every other university response in the scan. Hybrid-school and homeschool co-op preparation remain none found. One near-miss we checked and did not count: the Micro Schools Network certificate pairs Harvard's general Instructional Leadership Certificate with a microschool course written by MSN. Harvard's part is not built for microschools, so Harvard's coding is unchanged. Before we write "none found" for a university, we now search its K–12, online and outreach units, not only its education school. Wording narrowed October 28, 2026: our first version said ASU trains people to "open or teach in" a microschool. The fellowship is built for founders, so the site now says it helps people open one, and "none" now reads "none found."

Correction 02 · August 2026

We coded the University of West Florida "nothing found." It is a registered charter school sponsor.

Our July pass read only UWF's School of Education program roster. UWF is listed in the state's Directory of Charter School Sponsoring Entities, and its trustees approved it as authorizer of a Somerset Academy elementary school. The row is now coded built for the sector. The state sponsor registry is now checked before any institution is coded "nothing found." That change also surfaced Miami Dade College and Pasco-Hernando State College.

Correction 01 · July 2026

We claimed no one had scanned colleges of education for choice-sector programs. EdChoice had.

Surfing the Pipeline by Michael Q. McShane (October 2023) scanned Florida, Arizona, Wisconsin and Vermont, and is the source of the 34% preparedness figure. Our baseline now builds on it with attribution.
