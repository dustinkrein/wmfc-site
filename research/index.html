Florida, the ESA states, and the contrast

Ten states where K–12 money follows the student to private options through an ESA, then voucher-only states and states where it does not follow at all. One dot per institution, colored by what it has built. Tap a dot to see why it is there.

Florida: every state university and major private institution, 3 of 28 state colleges, and every college checked against the state charter sponsor registry. In the other nine: the flagship and the original state normal school, plus any institution found on the state’s charter-authorizer list or ESA provider list. Page-level reads, October 2026. A ringed dot sells courses or schooling to ESA families. A square is a summary row standing for several institutions not yet read one by one.

In the ESA states, universities show up as sellers, not trainers.

Several sell courses or online schooling to ESA families. What they sell is K–12 instruction. None of it is educator preparation.

Outside Michigan, one program was built for the sector.

Vanderbilt’s M.Ed. in Independent School Leadership, running since 2014, eleven years before Tennessee passed its ESA. It leads to no license and serves established private schools.
